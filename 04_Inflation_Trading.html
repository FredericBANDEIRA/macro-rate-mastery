Inflation Trading

TIPS mechanics, breakeven inflation rates, real yield curves, zero-coupon inflation swaps (ZCIS), the cash-swap basis, and inflation risk premia trading.

📐 The Fisher Decomposition of Sovereign Yields

Every nominal government bond yield ($y_{\text{nom}}$) can be decomposed into three distinct economic building blocks:

The Extended Fisher Identity
$$y_{\text{nom}} = r_{\text{real}} + \pi^e + \text{IRP}$$

Where:

  • $r_{\text{real}}$: The Real Yield, directly observed from Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS). Reflects pure real growth expectations and the scarcity of real capital.
  • $\pi^e$: Expected Inflation over the life of the bond.
  • $\text{IRP}$: The Inflation Risk Premium, the extra yield investors demand to bear the uncertainty of future purchasing power erosion.

The Breakeven Inflation Rate (BEI)

The Breakeven Inflation Rate is the spread between the yield of a nominal Treasury and an equal-maturity TIPS:

Breakeven Inflation Formula
$$\text{BEI} = y_{\text{nom}} - r_{\text{real}} = \pi^e + \text{IRP}$$
💡
The Breakeven Hurdle: If actual cumulative headline CPI inflation over the bond's life averages higher than the BEI at purchase, TIPS outperform the nominal bond. If realized inflation falls below the BEI, the nominal bond outperforms.

The 5y5y Forward Inflation Rate

Front-end breakevens (e.g. 2Y) are heavily distorted by oil prices, supply bottlenecks, and near-term CPI base effects. To evaluate true long-term inflation anchoring, macro strategists compute the 5-Year, 5-Year Forward Inflation Expectation Rate ($F_{5,5}$):

5y5y Forward Inflation Approximation
$$F_{5,5} \approx 2 \times \text{BEI}_{10\text{Y}} - \text{BEI}_{5\text{Y}}$$

If the 5Y BEI is $2.20\%$ and the 10Y BEI is $2.40\%$, the market-implied expected inflation over the 5-year period starting 5 years from now is $2 \times 2.40\% - 2.20\% = 2.60\%$.

📜 Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) Mechanics

Unlike standard nominal Treasuries where coupons and principal are fixed in dollar terms, TIPS provide direct purchasing power protection by indexing the bond's Principal Value to the non-seasonally adjusted US Consumer Price Index (CPI-U).

Principal Adjustment & Cash Flows

TIPS Principal Indexation
$$\text{Index Ratio}_t = \frac{\text{Ref CPI}_t}{\text{Ref CPI}_{\text{base}}}$$ $$\text{Adjusted Principal}_t = \text{Base Par Value} \times \text{Index Ratio}_t$$ $$\text{Semi-Annual Coupon}_t = \frac{c_{\text{real}}}{2} \times \text{Adjusted Principal}_t$$

Key Structural Features of TIPS

Feature Mechanism Desk Trading Implication
3-Month CPI Lag $\text{Ref CPI}_t$ is calculated via daily linear interpolation of the CPI-U figures published 3 months and 2 months prior. Creates predictable "carry" when near-term CPI prints are known. Traders front-run known inflation prints.
The Deflation Floor At maturity, the Treasury pays $\max(\text{Adjusted Principal}_T, \text{Base Par})$. Acts as an embedded put option on cumulative inflation. Newly issued TIPS trade at a slight premium during deflationary scares.
Phantom Income Tax In taxable accounts, annual upward adjustments to principal are taxed as ordinary income even before the bond matures. Concentrates TIPS ownership in tax-exempt entities (pensions, endowments, central banks).

🔄 Zero-Coupon Inflation Swaps (ZCIS)

While cash TIPS represent an indexed debt security, the interbank derivative market trades Zero-Coupon Inflation Swaps (ZCIS). In a ZCIS, party A pays a fixed compounded rate ($K$), and party B pays the actual realized percentage change in CPI over the tenor $T$:

Zero-Coupon Inflation Swap Settlement
$$\text{Fixed Leg Payout} = \text{Notional} \times \left[ (1 + K)^T - 1 \right]$$ $$\text{Floating Leg Payout} = \text{Notional} \times \left[ \frac{\text{CPI}_T}{\text{CPI}_0} - 1 \right]$$

The Cash-Swap Basis

In theory, the Breakeven rate of cash TIPS and the ZCIS swap rate should be identical. In practice, the swap rate almost always trades higher than the cash breakeven rate:

The Cash-Swap Basis Formula
$$\text{Basis} = \text{ZCIS Swap Rate} - \text{Cash TIPS Breakeven}$$
Driver of the Cash-Swap Basis Why it Widens the Spread
TIPS Liquidity Premium Cash TIPS are less liquid than off-the-run nominal Treasuries. Investors demand higher real yields on TIPS, which artificially depresses cash breakevens.
Balance Sheet & Financing Costs Buying cash TIPS requires balance sheet capacity and repo funding. Derivative swaps are unfunded and require only margin collateral.
End-User Demand Asymmetry Corporations and liability-driven pension funds (LDI) prefer paying fixed on inflation swaps to hedge long-dated liabilities without selling cash assets.
⚠️
The March 2020 Liquidity Crisis: During the pandemic market crash, primary dealers faced severe balance sheet constraints. 10Y Cash TIPS breakevens collapsed to 0.55%, while 10Y Inflation Swaps traded at 1.15%. The cash-swap basis blew out to an unprecedented 60 bps! Hedge funds that put on the basis trade (Long Cash TIPS / Pay Fixed Swap) generated massive alpha as the Fed intervened.

🎯 The 4 Core Inflation Desk Strategies

Global macro rates desks deploy four primary trade structures to monetize views on inflation, central bank credibility, and liquidity dislocations:

Strategy Position Structure Macro Catalyst DV01 / Risk Profile
1. Outright Breakeven Trade Long TIPS / Short Nominal Treasury Commodity surge, supply chain blockages, sticky wage inflation exceeding consensus. Duration-Neutral (DV01 = 0). Immune to parallel rate shifts; pure bet on $\Delta \text{BEI}$.
2. Real Yield Directional Bet Outright Long or Short TIPS Shift in the natural real neutral interest rate ($r^*$) or aggressive economic growth re-rating. Full real duration risk. Unhedged against nominal yields.
3. Breakeven Curve Trades Long 2Y Breakeven / Short 10Y Breakeven (Curve Flattener) Immediate geopolitical oil spike that central banks will aggressively fight in the medium term. Curve-neutral. Front end expands violently while long end stays anchored by monetary credibility.
4. Cash-Swap Basis Arbitrage Long Cash TIPS / Pay Fixed ZCIS Swap Market panic causes TIPS liquidity discount to blow out to unsustainable levels. Zero duration and zero inflation risk. Pure convergence trade as liquidity normalizes.

🎯 Inflation Trading & TIPS Interview Masterclass

Institutional interviewers on macro rates desks, global macro hedge funds, and sovereign bond pods probe deeply into inflation indexation lags, real yield curve dynamics, and the cash-swap basis. Click each card to reveal the desk breakdown.

Q01
"10Y nominal Treasuries yield 4.50% and 10Y TIPS yield 2.10%. Walk me through the exact decomposition of the 2.40% breakeven rate and what it tells you about market expectations."
▼

Desk Answer: The 2.40% breakeven spread ($4.50\% - 2.10\%$) is not pure expected inflation. By the Fisher decomposition, it consists of two distinct components:

$$\text{BEI}_{10\text{Y}} = \mathbb{E}[\pi_{10\text{Y}}] + \text{Inflation Risk Premium (IRP)} - \text{TIPS Liquidity Discount}$$
  • Expected Inflation ($\mathbb{E}[\pi]$): The market's consensus forecast for annualized headline CPI over the next decade (~2.15%).
  • Inflation Risk Premium (IRP): Extra yield investors demand to bear the purchasing power uncertainty of holding nominal fixed cash flows (+35 bps).
  • TIPS Liquidity Discount: Because TIPS are slightly less liquid than nominal Treasuries, their real yield is ~10 bps higher than fair value, which depresses the observed breakeven by 10 bps.
Bottom Line: If survey expectations (e.g. Michigan 5Y-10Y or SPF) are 2.20%, the remaining ~20 bps in the breakeven rate represents the net inflation risk premium.
Q02
"How does the 3-month CPI indexation lag create a seasonal carry trade in TIPS? How do macro traders position across Q1 vs Q4?"
▼

Desk Answer: TIPS principal is indexed to Non-Seasonally Adjusted (NSA) CPI-U with a 2-to-3 month interpolation lag:

  • Strong Seasonal Carry (Q1 / Spring): Historically, US prices rise rapidly in January, February, and March (apparel price resets, annual fee updates, energy rebound). Because of the lag, this high inflation is applied to TIPS principal indexation during March, April, and May, delivering massive positive carry (sometimes annualized at 5–8%).
  • Weak Seasonal Carry (Q4 / Autumn): In autumn (September–November), gasoline prices drop and holiday discounting sets in. NSA CPI frequently prints negative. TIPS principal shrinks or stays flat in November, December, and January, generating negative carry.
Desk Execution: Macro desks systematically go Long Front-End Breakevens in late winter to harvest seasonal carry, and roll out or short breakevens in late summer before the autumn deflationary prints hit the index ratio.
Q03
"Why do central banks watch the 5y5y forward inflation rate ($F_{5,5}$) rather than the 5Y or 10Y spot breakeven?"
▼

Desk Answer: Spot breakevens over the 1Y to 5Y horizon are heavily contaminated by transitory cyclical noise: commodity price shocks, supply disruptions, and fiscal stimulus checks:

  • The 5y5y forward rate strips out the immediate 5-year cyclical horizon and measures expected inflation between Year 6 and Year 10: $$F_{5,5} \approx 2 \times \text{BEI}_{10\text{Y}} - \text{BEI}_{5\text{Y}}$$
  • Over a horizon 5 to 10 years out, current transitory supply shocks will have completely decayed. Therefore, $F_{5,5}$ represents the market's assessment of pure central bank monetary credibility.
Policy Threshold: If 5Y breakevens spike to 3.5% due to an oil shock but 5y5y forwards remain glued to 2.25%, the Fed can afford to "look through" the shock. If 5y5y forwards climb above 2.75%, the Fed knows its credibility is slipping and will hike aggressively.
Q04
"What is the Cash-Swap Basis in the inflation market, and what happened to it during the March 2020 liquidity shock?"
▼

Desk Answer: The Cash-Swap Basis is the difference between the derivative Zero-Coupon Inflation Swap (ZCIS) rate and the cash TIPS breakeven rate ($\text{Basis} = \text{Swap} - \text{Cash}$):

  • In normal times, the basis trades between +10 and +20 bps due to the TIPS liquidity premium and the convenience of unfunded swap contracts.
  • In March 2020, as the pandemic escalated, a global scramble for US Dollar cash forced leveraged hedge funds to liquidate funded cash assets. Primary dealers' balance sheets became clogged with inventory and hit regulatory leverage limits (SLR).
  • Dealers could not absorb cash TIPS sales, causing cash real yields to surge and 10Y cash breakevens to plunge to 0.55%. Meanwhile, 10Y inflation swaps only dropped to 1.15%. The basis exploded to +60 bps!
The Arbitrage: The dislocation had nothing to do with inflation fundamentals—it was pure balance sheet scarcity. Once the Fed launched unlimited QE and exempted Treasuries from the Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR), the basis normalized back to 15 bps.
Q05
"Walk me through setting up a $50k DV01 duration-neutral long breakeven trade using 10Y nominal Treasuries and 10Y TIPS."
▼

Desk Answer: To construct a pure breakeven position that is immune to parallel shifts in real rates:

  • Leg 1 (Long TIPS): Buy 10Y TIPS to capture +$50,000 DV01. If the 10Y TIPS has a modified duration of 8.2 years and price of 100, its DV01 per $10k face is $8.20. You purchase: $$\text{Notional}_{\text{TIPS}} = \frac{\$50,000}{\$8.20 / \$10,000} \approx \$61.0\text{M}$$
  • Leg 2 (Short Nominal Treasury): Short 10Y nominal notes to sell -$50,000 DV01. If the 10Y nominal note has a modified duration of 7.8 years and price of 100, its DV01 per $10k face is $7.80. You short: $$\text{Notional}_{\text{Nominal}} = \frac{\$50,000}{\$7.80 / \$10,000} \approx \$64.1\text{M}$$
Trade P&L: If global yields rise by 50 bps in parallel (both nominal and real yields rise by 50 bps), your net P&L is approximately zero ($-\$2.5\text{M} + \$2.5\text{M} = 0$). If inflation expectations rise and the breakeven widens by 15 bps, your profit is exactly: $\text{DV01} \times \Delta \text{BEI} = \$50,000 \times 15 = +\$750,000$.
