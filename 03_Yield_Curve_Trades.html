Yield Curve Trades

Steepeners, flatteners, butterfly curvature trades, barbell vs. bullet strategies, and DV01 duration-neutral execution across the sovereign term structure.

📈 Principal Component Decomposition of the Yield Curve

Empirical studies across global fixed income markets show that ~99% of all yield curve movements can be decomposed into three orthogonal statistical factors via Principal Component Analysis (PCA):

PCA Factor Variance Explained Description Desk Trade Expression
PC1: Level (Parallel Shift) ~75% – 85% All yields along the curve shift upward or downward by roughly equal amounts. Driven by broad inflation shocks and global interest rate cycles. Outright Duration (Long / Short bond futures or 10Y benchmarks).
PC2: Slope (Steepening / Flattening) ~12% – 18% The front end moves in the opposite direction or with much greater magnitude than the long end. Driven by central bank policy stance relative to long-run potential growth. Duration-neutral curve trades: 2s10s, 5s30s, 2s30s curve spreads.
PC3: Curvature (Twist / Butterfly) ~3% – 5% The belly (3Y–7Y) of the curve moves relative to the outer wings (2Y and 10Y/30Y). Driven by uncertainty over terminal rates and timing of policy turning points. Butterflies: 2s5s10s, 3s7s10s, 5s10s30s.

The Four Canonical Yield Curve Shifts

Every macro rates trade is classified into one of four regimes depending on whether yields are rising (Bear) or falling (Bull), and whether the spread is expanding (Steepener) or contracting (Flattener):

Regime Yield Move Direction Spread Behavior ($10\text{Y} - 2\text{Y}$) Underlying Macro Catalyst Optimal Trade Expression
Bull Steepener Yields FALL ($\Delta y < 0$) Spread widens ($\Delta y_{2\text{Y}} \ll \Delta y_{10\text{Y}} < 0$) Recession onset, emergency central bank cuts, banking distress (flight to front-end cash). Long 2Y / Short 10Y (DV01-neutral). Front end surges in price as rate cuts are aggressively priced.
Bear Steepener Yields RISE ($\Delta y > 0$) Spread widens ($\Delta y_{10\text{Y}} \gg \Delta y_{2\text{Y}} > 0$) Fiscal expansion / debt supply deluge, rising term premia, re-accelerating long-run inflation. Long 2Y / Short 10Y or 30Y. Long end cheapens under sovereign bond auction indigestion.
Bull Flattener Yields FALL ($\Delta y < 0$) Spread narrows ($\Delta y_{10\text{Y}} \ll \Delta y_{2\text{Y}} < 0$) Aggressive disinflation, global growth slowdown, flight to safety into long-duration paper. Short 2Y / Long 10Y or 30Y. Long bonds rally aggressively as terminal growth expectations crater.
Bear Flattener Yields RISE ($\Delta y > 0$) Spread narrows ($\Delta y_{2\text{Y}} \gg \Delta y_{10\text{Y}} > 0$) Central bank aggressive rate hike cycle to combat inflation. Curve frequently inverts ($2\text{s}10\text{s} < 0$). Short 2Y / Long 10Y. Front end sells off violently as rate hikes are pulled forward.
💡
The Golden Rule of Curve Trading: "Bulls cut rates, Bears hike rates. Steepeners occur when the front end falls more (Bull) or long end rises more (Bear). Flatteners occur when the front end rises more (Bear) or long end falls more (Bull)."

⚖️ Duration-Neutral Curve Construction & DV01 Weighting

A fatal beginner error is trading equal notional amounts on both legs (e.g. Long $10M 2Y vs. Short $10M 10Y). A 10Y Treasury note has roughly 4 times the interest rate sensitivity (DV01) of a 2Y note. An equal-notional trade is simply an outright directional duration bet.

DV01 Definition & The Hedge Ratio
$$\text{DV01} = \left| \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta y (1 \text{ bp})} \right| \approx P \times \text{ModDuration} \times 0.0001$$ $$\text{Hedge Ratio} = \frac{\text{DV01}_{2\text{Y}}}{\text{DV01}_{10\text{Y}}} \quad \implies \quad \text{Notional}_{2\text{Y}} = \text{Notional}_{10\text{Y}} \times \frac{\text{DV01}_{10\text{Y}}}{\text{DV01}_{2\text{Y}}}$$

Carry & Roll-Down: The Cost of Holding Curve Trades

When you hold a curve position, your P&L is not just the shift in the curve. You must account for:

  • Carry: The net coupon income received on the long leg minus the repo borrowing cost paid to fund the short leg.
  • Roll-Down: The price gain resulting from bonds shortening in maturity along an upward-sloping or inverted yield curve over time.
⚠️
The Negative Carry Trap: Entering a steepener when the curve is deeply inverted entails substantial negative carry. The front end yields 5.25% while the 10Y yields 4.25%, meaning your long 2Y leg yields more, but shorting 10Y forces you to pay repo while borrowing at the inverted short-term rate. If the curve fails to steepen quickly, carry and roll-down bleed your P&L.

🦋 Butterfly Trades: Trading Curvature (Belly vs. Wings)

A butterfly trade isolates the curvature (PC3) of the yield curve while hedging out both level (PC1) and slope (PC2). It consists of a position in the "belly" (intermediate maturity) hedged against opposite positions in the two "wings" (shorter and longer maturities).

The Canonical 2s5s10s Butterfly Spread
$$\text{Fly Spread} = -y_{2\text{Y}} + 2 y_{5\text{Y}} - y_{10\text{Y}} = (y_{5\text{Y}} - y_{2\text{Y}}) - (y_{10\text{Y}} - y_{5\text{Y}})$$

Structure of a Butterfly Position

Butterfly Strategy Leg Structure When to Execute (View) Profit Driver
Short the Belly / Long Wings (Buy the Fly) Long 2Y + Long 10Y (Wings) / Short 5Y (Body) Belly is rich (yield too low, price too high relative to wings). Expecting curve to become more humped. 5Y yield rises relative to 2Y and 10Y; Fly spread widens.
Long the Belly / Short Wings (Sell the Fly) Short 2Y + Short 10Y (Wings) / Long 5Y (Body) Belly is cheap (yield too high relative to wings). Expecting belly yields to compress. 5Y yield falls relative to 2Y and 10Y; Fly spread narrows.

50/50 Fly vs. Regression-Weighted Fly

The standard 50/50 butterfly gives 50% DV01 weight to each wing. However, because changes in slope correlate with changes in curvature, a pure 50/50 fly is not truly slope-neutral. Rates desks calculate the regression beta ($\beta$) between the 2s5s curve and the 5s10s curve:

$$\Delta (y_{5\text{Y}} - y_{2\text{Y}}) = \alpha + \beta \Delta (y_{10\text{Y}} - y_{5\text{Y}}) + \epsilon_t \quad \implies \quad w_{2\text{Y}} = \frac{\beta}{1 + \beta}, \quad w_{10\text{Y}} = \frac{1}{1 + \beta}$$

🏋️ Barbell vs. Bullet: The Convexity Trade-Off

A classic structural decision in portfolio management and rates trading is whether to match duration using a Bullet (a single intermediate bond, e.g. 10Y) or a Barbell (a combination of short and long bonds, e.g. 2Y and 30Y).

The Convexity Advantage of the Barbell
$$D_{\text{barbell}} = w_1 D_1 + w_2 D_2 = D_{\text{bullet}}$$ $$C_{\text{barbell}} = w_1 C_1 + w_2 C_2 > C_{\text{bullet}} \quad \text{since } C \propto D^2$$

Because convexity increases approximately with the square of duration, the 30Y bond provides disproportionate convexity. A duration-matched 2Y/30Y barbell has strictly greater positive convexity than a 10Y bullet.

Convexity is Not Free: The Price-Yield Payoff

Market Environment Barbell Performance Bullet Performance Winning Strategy
Large Volatility / Big Rate Moves ($\pm 100\text{ bps}$) Gains more on rallies, loses less on selloffs due to positive convexity ($\frac{1}{2} C (\Delta y)^2$). Lower convexity dampens price upside on large rallies and amplifies downside on large selloffs. Barbell Wins
Calm / Range-Bound Rates Market ($\Delta y \approx 0$) Bleeds carry and roll-down. Negative roll-down along a concave curve eats into returns. Captures maximum steep roll-down in the 7Y–10Y belly of the curve without paying for excess convexity. Bullet Wins

🎯 Yield Curve Trading Interview Masterclass

These scenarios reflect real-world questions asked on investment bank rates trading desks, hedge fund macro pods, and asset management portfolio teams. Click each question to reveal the desk-level breakdown.

Q01
"You want to set up a $100k DV01 2s10s steepener. The 2Y note has a DV01 of $1.85 per $10k face, and the 10Y note has a DV01 of $8.00 per $10k face. Calculate the exact notional amounts on each leg."
▼

Desk Answer: For a steepener, you go Long 2Y (profit when 2Y yield drops) and Short 10Y (profit when 10Y yield rises or falls by less):

  • 10Y Leg Notional:
    $$\text{Notional}_{10\text{Y}} = \frac{\text{Target DV01}}{\text{DV01}_{10\text{Y}} / \text{Face}} = \frac{\$100,000}{\$8.00 / \$10,000} = \frac{\$100,000}{0.0008} = \$125,000,000 \quad (\$125\text{M})$$
  • 2Y Leg Notional:
    $$\text{Notional}_{2\text{Y}} = \frac{\text{Target DV01}}{\text{DV01}_{2\text{Y}} / \text{Face}} = \frac{\$100,000}{\$1.85 / \$10,000} = \frac{\$100,000}{0.000185} \approx \$540,540,540 \quad (\$540.5\text{M})$$
  • Hedge Ratio:
    $$\frac{\text{Notional}_{2\text{Y}}}{\text{Notional}_{10\text{Y}}} = \frac{\$540.5\text{M}}{\$125\text{M}} \approx 4.32$$
Desk Takeaway: You must buy $540.5M of 2Y notes for every $125M of 10Y notes sold short. If the 2s10s spread widens by 10 bps, your gross P&L is: $\text{Target DV01} \times \Delta \text{Spread} = \$100,000 \times 10 = +\$1,000,000$.
Q02
"Explain why an inverted yield curve makes holding a steepener an expensive 'negative carry' trade, and how roll-down impacts the position."
▼

Desk Answer: In an inverted yield curve (e.g. 2Y at 5.00%, 10Y at 4.00%):

  • Carry Component: To short $125M of 10Y notes, you must borrow them in repo and invest the proceeds. In an inverted curve, financing rates (repo/SOFR) are at the high front-end rate (5.25%), meaning you pay more to borrow the long bond than the coupon it yields.
  • Roll-Down Component: As time passes, bonds roll down the curve toward shorter maturities. On an inverted curve, rolling down means the yield rises as maturity shortens. A rising yield causes capital losses on your long 2Y leg!
Trader Insight: A steepener on an inverted curve bleeds cash every single day it stays flat. Traders refer to this as the "steepener bleed". You must have high conviction on the timing of the Fed cutting cycle to justify the negative carry.
Q03
"What is the difference between a 50/50 butterfly and a regression-weighted butterfly? Why would a rates desk prefer the regression fly?"
▼

Desk Answer: In a 2s5s10s butterfly, a 50/50 fly allocates 50% of the body's DV01 to the 2Y wing and 50% to the 10Y wing:

  • The Flaw of 50/50: In real markets, the 2s5s slope moves with roughly 1.3x to 1.6x the volatility of the 5s10s slope. When the overall curve steepens or flattens, a 50/50 butterfly experiences directional slope leakage (P&L changes even though curvature hasn't moved).
  • The Regression Fly: A regression-weighted fly regresses historical changes in 2s5s against 5s10s to estimate beta ($\beta$): $$\Delta(y_{5\text{Y}} - y_{2\text{Y}}) = \alpha + \beta \Delta(y_{10\text{Y}} - y_{5\text{Y}})$$ The wing weights are set according to beta ($w_{2\text{Y}} = \frac{\beta}{1+\beta}, w_{10\text{Y}} = \frac{1}{1+\beta}$).
Takeaway: A regression butterfly is genuinely slope-neutral. It allows the desk to isolate pure curvature (belly rich/cheap) without taking accidental risk on whether the 2s10s curve is steepening or flattening.
Q04
"How does the US Treasury's Quarterly Refunding Announcement (QRA) impact the 5s30s and 10s30s curve spreads?"
▼

Desk Answer: The Treasury's QRA dictates the exact auction sizes for 2Y, 3Y, 5Y, 7Y, 10Y, 20Y, and 30Y debt over the coming quarter:

  • If the Treasury increases long-duration issuance (10Y, 30Y) beyond primary dealer expectations, the massive supply creates duration indigestion. Long-end yields must rise to entice marginal buyers (pensions, foreign accounts).
  • This causes a Bear Steepener in 5s30s and 10s30s as term premia expand on the long end.
  • Conversely, if the Treasury tilts issuance toward short-dated T-bills (as occurred in late 2023 under Janet Yellen), long-duration supply pressure evaporates, leading to a Bull Flattener in 10s30s.
Rule of Thumb: Heavy coupon supply announcements steepen 5s30s, while bill-heavy financing flatten 5s30s and 10s30s.
Q05
"Under what market regime does a Barbell outperform an equal-duration Bullet, and why is convexity referred to as an 'option-like' feature?"
▼

Desk Answer: Convexity measures the second derivative of bond price with respect to yield ($\frac{d^2 P}{dy^2}$).

  • Because positive convexity means the price-yield curve is strictly convex to the origin, the price rises more when yields fall by 100 bps than it drops when yields rise by 100 bps: $$\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D \Delta y + \frac{1}{2} C (\Delta y)^2$$ Notice that the convexity term ($\frac{1}{2} C (\Delta y)^2$) is always positive regardless of whether $\Delta y > 0$ or $\Delta y < 0$!
  • This acts like an embedded long straddle option: whenever rates move violently in either direction, the Barbell's superior convexity generates positive alpha over the Bullet.
The Cost: In a tranquil, range-bound market, the option expires worthless—the Barbell loses money to the Bullet through lower yield and inferior roll-down.
